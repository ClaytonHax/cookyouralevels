# Ethics and Ownership
> Answers must be applied to the scenario given; examiners reward balanced evaluation (benefit and harm) and correct licence terms.

## Key ideas

### Professional ethics and societies
- Computing professionals make decisions that affect privacy, safety, jobs and security, and the law cannot cover everything. Ethics gives a standard of right behaviour.
- Professional bodies (BCS, IEEE) publish codes of conduct: act in the public interest, be competent and honest, respect privacy and confidentiality, respect intellectual property, avoid conflicts of interest and keep skills up to date.
- Membership lets bodies set standards and discipline members who breach them.

### Judging ethical and unethical behaviour
- Consider all stakeholders: users, the public, employees, employer, shareholders.
- Unethical behaviour example: selling personal data without consent; releasing software known to be faulty; using code without permission. Effects: loss of privacy, financial and safety harm, loss of trust, legal action and reputational damage.
- Ethical behaviour example: reporting a vulnerability to the vendor, testing thoroughly, refusing to build misleading features. Effects: trust, safer products, fewer legal risks.
- Evaluate using the code: was the public interest served? Was consent given? Was anyone harmed?

### Copyright
- Copyright law gives creators legal ownership of original works (software, music, images, text) so others cannot copy, distribute or modify them without permission.
- It encourages creation by letting creators earn from their work; it prevents piracy, plagiarism and unauthorised use. Software copyright is breached by copying, sharing licence keys, or running more copies than licensed.

### Software licences
| Licence | Key features |
|---|---|
| Commercial | Bought; no source code; cannot copy or modify; support and updates |
| Shareware | Free trial for limited time or features; pay to continue/unlock; no modification |
| Freeware | Free to use, but source code not provided and cannot be modified |
| Free software (Free Software Foundation) | Users may run, study, share and modify; source provided; derivative works must remain free |
| Open source (Open Source Initiative) | Source code available to view, modify and distribute; community improvements; may allow commercial reuse |

- Choosing: a business needing support and warranty may pick commercial; a school wanting flexibility and low cost may use open source; a developer wanting to test before buying uses shareware.

### Artificial intelligence
- AI covers systems that perform tasks needing human-like intelligence: recommendation systems, speech recognition, image recognition, robotics, medical diagnosis, self-driving vehicles, chatbots.
- Social impact: personalised services and safety gains but bias, loss of privacy, deepfakes and reduced human contact. Economic impact: productivity and new industries but job losses and skills gaps; costs concentrate with large firms. Environmental impact: optimising energy use and resources but high energy consumption for training and data centres, plus e-waste.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Moral principles governing a person's or group's behaviour. |
| Code of conduct | Published rules describing professional standards. |
| Copyright | Legal right of the creator to control copying and use of their work. |
| Open source | Software whose source code is freely available to view, change and share. |
| Shareware | Software distributed free for a trial period, with payment required for continued use. |
| Piracy | Illegal copying or distribution of copyrighted software. |
| Artificial intelligence | Computer systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence. |

## Worked example
**Question:** A developer finds a security hole in her employer's software but is told to ignore it to meet a release deadline. Discuss using the BCS/IEEE code. [5]

**Model answer:**
- Code requires acting in the public interest and protecting users (1).
- Releasing with a known flaw could expose users' data, causing harm and legal liability (1).
- She should report concerns through proper channels and document them (1).
- Ignoring it would be unethical; delay is costly but a breach damages reputation and trust far more (1).
- Balanced conclusion: fix first, or release with the flaw disclosed and mitigated (1).

## Common mistakes
- Saying copyright "stops all copying" without noting it can be licensed.
- Confusing freeware with free (open) software.
- Treating open source as "not copyrighted" or never commercial.
- Giving one-sided answers on AI.
- Writing generically about ethics without referencing the scenario.

## Exam tips
- "Discuss/Evaluate" requires both sides and a conclusion.
- Link the licence feature (source code, modification rights, cost) to the user's need.
- Use named examples of AI applications, not just "AI helps people".
- Mention a code-of-conduct principle by name (public interest, competence, confidentiality).

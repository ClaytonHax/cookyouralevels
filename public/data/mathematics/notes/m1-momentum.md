# Momentum
> Examiners want you to fix a positive direction, write the conservation equation with correct signs for every velocity, and interpret a negative answer as a reversal.

## Key ideas

### Momentum
Momentum is mass times velocity, $p=mv$. It is a vector, so in one dimension it has a sign: choose one direction as positive and give every velocity (and so every momentum) a sign relative to it. The unit is $\mathrm{N\,s}$ (equivalently $\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$).

### Conservation of momentum in direct collisions
When two particles collide directly (along the line joining their centres of motion) and no external horizontal force acts, the total momentum just before equals the total momentum just after:
$$m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$$
Method:
1. Draw a "before" and "after" diagram with masses and velocity arrows.
2. State the positive direction.
3. Write the conservation equation, substituting velocities with signs.
4. Solve for the unknown. A negative result means that particle moves in the negative direction.

Collisions between smooth objects obey this law even if kinetic energy is lost. Kinetic energy is generally *not* conserved in a collision, so do not use it unless told the collision is perfectly elastic (which the syllabus does not expect you to assume).

### Coalescing particles
If two particles stick together they move as a single body of mass $m_1+m_2$ with a common velocity $v$:
$$m_1u_1+m_2u_2=(m_1+m_2)v$$
This is the one-unknown case and is usually the easiest. Words to recognise: "coalesce", "join together", "stick together", "become attached".

### Rebounding and separating particles
Where the particles bounce apart, they have different final velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$. The conservation equation has two unknowns, so a second piece of information is supplied, such as one particle's final velocity, a relationship between speeds ("the speed of $B$ is twice that of $A$"), or that one particle is brought to rest. A particle that "rebounds" has reversed direction, so its final velocity has the opposite sign to its initial velocity.

### Momentum in one dimension with sign conventions
- Velocity in the positive direction is positive; the opposite direction is negative.
- If particles approach each other, one of the initial velocities is negative.
- If the question gives *speeds* (no direction), you decide the signs; state your convention.
- After solving, translate signs back into words, for example "$A$ reverses direction with speed $1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$".

## Formulae
- $p=mv$.
- Conservation: $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$.
- Coalescence: $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=(m_1+m_2)v$.
- **In MF19:** nothing on momentum (its only mechanics formulae are the four suvat equations). **Memorise** all of the above.
- Gravity, if needed in a related part of a question, is $g=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Worked example
**Question 1.** Particle $A$ of mass $3\ \mathrm{kg}$ moves at $4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and collides with particle $B$ of mass $5\ \mathrm{kg}$ at rest. They coalesce. Find the common speed.

Positive direction: direction of $A$'s motion.
$$\begin{aligned}3(4)+5(0)&=(3+5)v\\12&=8v\\v&=1.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\end{aligned}$$

**Question 2.** Particles $P$ (mass $2\ \mathrm{kg}$, speed $5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$) and $Q$ (mass $3\ \mathrm{kg}$, speed $2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$) move towards each other along a straight line and collide directly. After the collision, $P$ moves with speed $1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in the opposite direction to its original motion. Find the velocity of $Q$ after the collision.

Positive direction: the original direction of $P$. Then $Q$'s initial velocity is $-2$ and $P$'s final velocity is $-1$.
$$\begin{aligned}2(5)+3(-2)&=2(-1)+3v\\10-6&=-2+3v\\4&=-2+3v\\v&=2\end{aligned}$$
$Q$ moves at $2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in the original direction of $P$ (so $Q$ also reverses).

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to give the approaching particle a negative velocity.
- Using $m_1+m_2$ on the right-hand side when the particles do not stick together.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved.
- Giving a final answer as "$-1.5$" with no statement of direction.
- Mixing up before and after quantities, especially when the diagram has arrows both ways.
- Using speed in the equation when the velocity is in the negative direction.

## Exam tips
- Always draw before/after diagrams and mark the positive direction with an arrow.
- Substitute with brackets around negative values to avoid sign slips: $3(-2)$, not $3-2$.
- Check your answer by plausibility: total momentum must match on both sides.
- Where the collision is followed by motion on a rough surface or against a wall, the momentum principle gives the speed *just after* the collision; then switch to Newton's laws or energy for what follows.
- Say clearly in words which way each particle moves after impact.
